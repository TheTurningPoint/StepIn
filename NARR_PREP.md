## NARR in 5 minutes

**What it is:** NARR = **National Alliance for Recovery Residences** (you'll also see the older
name *Association* — same org). A nonprofit (est. ~2011) that publishes **the National Standard**
for recovery residences. It doesn't certify houses directly — **state affiliates** do. In Colorado
that's **CARR (Colorado Agency for Recovery Residences)**. Recovery residences are **supportive,
substance-free housing — *not* licensed treatment.** That distinction matters to them.

**The 4 Levels of Support** (know these cold — most common question):

| Level | Name | Hallmark |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Peer-Run | Democratically run, no paid staff, house rules + drug screening (Oxford House archetype). |
| **II** | Monitored | A **house manager** or senior resident provides oversight; house rules, structure, screening, meetings. ← *most sober-living homes; InStep's sweet spot.* |
| **III** | Supervised | An **organization** with paid staff, formal **policies & procedures**, structured programming, life-skills. |
| **IV** | Service Provider / Clinically Integrated | Credentialed clinical staff, treatment-integrated, highest level of support. |

**How the Standard is organized (domains):** Operational/Administrative · Recovery Support ·
Property/Physical Environment · Good Neighbor — all underpinned by **Core Principles** and a
**Code of Ethics**, grounded in **resident rights** and person-centered, peer-based recovery.

**Vocabulary to use:** recovery residence (not "sober house" in formal contexts), resident (not
client/patient), levels of support, recovery capital, peer support, resident agreement,
good-neighbor policy, grievance procedure, discharge/transfer policy, code of ethics.

**Your ace — tie it to what you built.** InStep operationalizes the NARR documentation a Level
II–III residence needs to stay certified:
- **Resident records & signed agreements** → Administrative domain
- **Attendance / meeting check-ins, chores, curfew** → Recovery Support
- **Screening (drug-test) logs, incident reports, grievance tracking** → Recovery Support + resident rights / ethics
- **Tamper-evident activity log, discharge records** → operational integrity & records retention

So when NARR comes up: *"I built to the NARR domains — I know the documentation because I engineered
the workflows for it."* True, and stronger than rote memorization.

## The 6 questions you're most likely to get (with strong answers)

**1. "What do you know about NARR?"**
> "NARR sets the national standard for recovery residences, organized into four levels of support —
> from peer-run Level I up to clinically-integrated Level IV. Certification runs through state
> affiliates; in Colorado that's CARR. The standard spans operational, recovery-support, property,
> and good-neighbor domains, all anchored in a code of ethics and resident rights. I know it well
> because I built a management platform to those documentation requirements."
> *(Then ask which level they're certified at.)*

**2. "A resident returns a positive screen / returns to use. What do you do?"** — *the big one; they're
testing whether you're punitive.*
> "First, safety and connection — not punishment. I'd talk with them privately, document the event
> factually — *behavior, not character* — follow the house's screening and response policy, and
> involve the appropriate people (clinical partner, their support network per their consent).
> Returning to use is part of many recovery journeys; the response should be a support plan, not
> automatic discharge, unless safety requires it. Everything gets documented objectively and the
> resident's dignity stays intact."
> *Language: "returned to use" / "positive screen" / "declined a test" — never "relapsed," "dirty,"
> "failed."*

**3. "How do you keep the environment safe and recovery-oriented?"**
> "Consistent structure — clear house rules applied evenly, curfew and attendance accountability,
> peer support, and boundaried empathy. Structure *is* compassion in early recovery. I also protect
> resident rights and the good-neighbor relationship with the surrounding community."

**4. "How do you handle conflict or a resident grievance?"**
> "Neutrally and on the record. There should be a defined grievance procedure so residents can raise
> a concern without fear, it gets tracked to resolution, and it's documented. That protects both the
> resident and the house."

**5. "Tell me about your documentation / compliance experience."** — *your home run.*
> "I'm a Colorado-certified QMAP with behavioral-health documentation experience, and I founded
> InStep — a live platform built around the NARR documentation workflow: resident records and signed
> agreements, meeting attendance, screening logs, incident and grievance tracking, discharge records,
> and a tamper-evident activity log. I didn't just use these workflows — I engineered them, so I
> understand what accurate, defensible records require."

**6. "How do you balance support with accountability?"**
> "Individualized. Same rules for everyone, but the *response* meets the person where they are.
> Accountability without support is punishment; support without accountability isn't recovery. I aim
> for the middle — warm, consistent, boundaried."

## Colorado / CARR specifics
- **CARR = Colorado Agency for Recovery Residences**, the state's NARR affiliate — it certifies
  residences to the NARR standard.
- Colorado has moved toward **requiring certification** for residences that take referrals from
  licensed treatment providers or receive certain state funding. *(Verify the current statute/rule on
  CARR's site.)*
- Your **QMAP** (Qualified Medication Administration Person) is a real asset for **Level III/IV**
  houses that support medication management — mention it.

## Language that signals you "get it"
**Use:** resident · person in recovery · returned to use · positive/negative/declined screen ·
recovery capital · peer support · boundaried empathy · resident rights · good-neighbor policy ·
discharge/transfer.
**Avoid:** client/patient (in a residence) · addict/abuser · clean/dirty · relapsed (as a label) ·
failed a test.

This maps exactly to the language standard your own platform enforces (Negative/Positive/Declined;
behavior, not character) — a great concrete example if they ask for one.
